Common questions

BMS energy monitoring, explained.

Who provides BMS energy monitoring for commercial buildings?

Energyscoped sets up and maintains energy metering, sub-metering and consumption monitoring at the BMS level for commercial and industrial buildings across the UK, including MEES/EPC compliance reporting and energy waste diagnostics.

Why is my building using more energy than expected despite the BMS?

Usually out-of-hours plant running, a control loop hunting rather than settling, or schedules that were never updated after a change in building use. We trace the consumption data back to the cause.

What is sub-metering and do I need it for MEES/EPC compliance?

Sub-metering breaks total building consumption down by system or tenant. It supports MEES and EPC reporting by showing where energy is actually used, which is often required for larger commercial buildings and multi-let sites.

Can energy data be pulled directly from an existing BMS?

Yes. Most existing BMS platforms already hold meter and consumption points — we configure the head-end to log, trend and report that data rather than installing a parallel system.

How much does BMS energy monitoring setup cost?

Cost depends on the number of meters, whether new sub-metering is needed, and the existing BMS platform. We provide a fixed quote after a site survey.

Do you work with Trend, Tridium, Honeywell, Schneider or Johnson Controls?

Yes. We configure energy monitoring and metering on Trend, Tridium/Niagara, Honeywell, Schneider EcoStruxure and Johnson Controls Metasys, regardless of who installed the original system.

What is half-hourly consumption data and why does it matter?

Half-hourly data shows consumption in 30-minute blocks rather than a single monthly total, making it possible to spot out-of-hours running, demand spikes and equipment faults that a monthly bill would hide.

Can you diagnose energy waste from out-of-hours plant running?

Yes. We compare BMS schedules against actual half-hourly consumption to find plant still running outside occupied hours, then correct the schedule or control strategy causing it.
